# Execution Log

Assumptions and judgement calls made **while executing** a step — the small decisions taken without
asking, the places where reality differed from the plan, and the things believed but not yet
verified.

## What belongs here, and what does not

| Register | Holds |
|---|---|
| [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) decisions log (`D-xx`) | Decisions **chosen by the user** in answer to a question, before execution |
| [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) findings (`R-xx`) | Risks that affect more than one phase |
| [docs/adr/](docs/adr/) | Architectural decisions with alternatives and a revisit condition |
| **This file** (`A-xxx`) | Everything settled **during** a step — assumptions, small choices, deviations, discoveries, corrections |

The distinction that matters: a `D-xx` was offered as a question and answered. An `A-xxx` was not
worth interrupting for, but is still the reason some piece of code looks the way it does.

## Conventions

- **IDs are permanent.** A superseded entry is marked, never renumbered or deleted.
- **Kind** is one of:
  - `assumption` — believed true, not verified. These carry risk.
  - `choice` — a judgement call where a reasonable alternative existed.
  - `deviation` — differs from the plan, the specification, or a stated intent.
  - `discovery` — learned during execution and changed something.
  - `correction` — something was wrong and was fixed.

## Step 8.3 — Fallback chain (§53)

| ID | Kind | What | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-598 | choice | The provider list IS the fallback chain — the first capable provider is tried, then the second, etc. | A separate per-task fallback config would duplicate the provider order already expressed in the list. | A-599 | choice | Non-retryable `ProviderError` propagates immediately, bypassing remaining candidates | A malformed request or context-length violation won't be fixed by switching providers — retrying wastes time and may produce a confusing error from a different provider. `retryable=False` is the provider's signal that the request itself is the problem. |

## Step 17.3 — The evidence budget made reachable

| ID | Type | Claim | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-777 | correction | A-773 was overstated: the class minimum is not what costs precision | It was inferred from a run of the *changed* classifier, where two questions were routed to AGGREGATION and its minimum of three pulled in passages scored −2.96 and −3.10. At HEAD both are DIRECT with a minimum of one, so the minimum never fires for them. Measured across all 18 pairs, the minimum overrides the margin exactly three times — and two of the three are hits. `reader-vs-writer` is the case `selector.py` defends in as many words: a comparison whose second source scored −0.790 against a top of +5.144, and it is the other half of the answer. The minimum costs one passage of precision across the whole set. |
| A-778 | discovery | The per-document cap decided the size of every evidence list, not the class budget | `max_chunks_per_document` is 4 and this library holds one document, so pruning cut 40 candidates to 4 on all eighteen questions, one stage before the component whose job that is. `CountRange(3, 8)` for SUMMARY, AGGREGATION, CONCEPT_MAP, MULTI_DOCUMENT and QUIZ_GENERATION could not reach 8, and neither could `max_items`. The selector sent four passages once in eighteen questions and never more. The pruner is class-aware in one direction only: it halves the cap for a comparison, which in a one-document library yields exactly two — the COMPARISON minimum — so the score margin had no say in a comparison at all. |
| A-779 | choice | The cap withdraws where there is only one document | The room it holds back is room for another document, so where every candidate came from the same one there is nothing to hold it back for. Left applying it is not a diversity rule but a truncation. Reverting to it was considered and rejected: the protection is illusory the moment a second document arrives, since the cap is per-document and two documents admit eight passages just as readily. Depending on an accident that stops working as the library grows is worse than the thing it was accidentally preventing. |
| A-780 | discovery | The relative margin cannot tell a flat-bad ranking from a flat-good one | The reason the change is measurable at all, and the more useful finding. *"What are the main conclusions of this report?"* returns eight passages scoring −8.012 down to −9.188 — relative distances of 0.00 to 0.147, every one of them inside the 0.35 margin. `studio-what-is` tops out at +7.99 and this at −8.01, and the margin reads both as a tight cluster worth keeping whole. The settings comment rules out an absolute cutoff on the grounds that it would discard every candidate; on a question the book does not answer, discarding every candidate is the right answer, which is the same gap A-769 names from the other side. |
| A-781 | discovery | Two shared models were being called from several threads at once | Found because it took the evaluation harness down mid-run. Retrieval embeds an expanded question with one `gather`, and each `embed_query` offloads to its own thread against the one `SentenceTransformer` the process holds; the tokeniser underneath is reconfigured on entry, and the second thread in aborts the call outright rather than reading a stale setting. It is a race, so it had been running for weeks without showing. The reranker has the identical hazard and is not reachable this way from a single request — only from two students asking at once, which is why nothing had caught it. | A-782 | correction | The evaluation scripts died partway through on typography the console could not print | `explain_retrieval.py` stopped at the second passage of eight, on a hyphen character the inherited console encoding has no mapping for, and printed a report that read as complete. A diagnostic that ends early and looks finished is worse than one that crashes. |
| A-783 | finding | Selection precision has an arithmetic ceiling of 1/n where the gold names one page | `report-conclusions` labels a single page, so sending four passages caps its precision at 0.25 and sending eight caps it at 0.125 — before anything about retrieval is considered. The mean fell 0.69 to 0.68 entirely through that one pair. This is the trap A-765 named for a fixed window reappearing a level up: a class budgeted for breadth is scored down in proportion to the breadth it was budgeted for. ## Step 18.1 — The typed API layer

| ID | Type | Claim | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-784 | discovery | The error contract has two shapes, and only one of them was designed | Domain errors reach a handler that returns a message and the trace id. Request-body validation never reaches those handlers — the web framework answers first, with a list of per-field objects and no trace id — because no handler is registered for it. Both arrive as **422**, so the status cannot tell them apart. A client modelling only the documented shape throws while handling the error rather than reporting it, and the shape it would throw on is the one a form produces on almost every wrong keystroke. Found by probing the running handlers rather than by reading them, and both shapes are now in the schema. |
| A-785 | choice | Three failure kinds, kept apart, because the response to each differs | The server refused; the server answered in a shape the schemas reject; the request never reached a server. One error type would leave every caller to re-derive which happened, and the middle one — the two sides of the contract having moved apart — is the one that would be read as a network blip and retried until it looked like an outage. A refusal is retried only where the server implied it might answer differently, a contract mismatch never, a network failure always. |
| A-786 | choice | The mirror keeps the backend's field names rather than renaming them | Every schema uses the wire's snake_case instead of the camelCase the rest of the frontend uses. A mirror earns its keep only if a drift between the two sides is visible by reading them side by side, and a translation layer hides exactly that: a field renamed on the server looks, on this side, like a field somebody forgot to map. |
| A-787 | correction | The query client's retry rule could never fire | It refused to retry when the error was a `Response` with status 401 or 404. Nothing in this codebase throws a `Response`, so the branch was unreachable and every authorization failure was retried three times — latency spent on a question whose answer cannot change, since a Knowledge Base another user owns answers 404 by design. Rewritten against the error types the client now throws. |
| A-788 | discovery | The trace header was set on every response and readable on none, across origins | The client reads the trace id from the header rather than the body, because the header is the only one of the two that is always there. But a browser hands script only a few headers unless the server names the rest, and `expose_headers` was never set — so cross-origin the header is invisible. A frontend behind a development proxy is same-origin and never notices, which puts the first failure in production and takes the identifier out of exactly the errors somebody is trying to trace. One line in the application setup, with a test that reads the real app rather than a locally assembled one. |
| A-789 | choice | The fixtures are captured backend output, not written by hand | Every response model was built and serialised, and the JSON pasted in verbatim. A fixture somebody invents describes the contract as they remember it, which makes it the same document being checked — and two properties of the real output are load-bearing and easy to misremember: an unset optional arrives as an explicit null rather than a missing key, and timestamps carry microsecond precision. The nullable-versus-optional distinction matters more than it looks under this project's strict optional-property setting, where guessing wrong forces callers to handle an `undefined` that never arrives while still leaving the null they will actually get. |
